# Prose Research Notes

Research date: 2026-06-04

Purpose: convert durable writing craft principles into practical skill rules for beautiful prose and anti-AI-slop editing.

## Sources Consulted

- George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" summary/source pointer via Orwell Foundation mirror: https://inigomedina.co/library/work/orwell-politics-and-the-english-language
- Paul Graham, "Write Simply": https://paulgraham.com/simply.html
- Purdue OWL, "Concision": https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/conciseness/index.html
- Purdue OWL, "Strategies for Variation": https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/index.html

## Findings Integrated

### 1. Clear writing is clear thinking

Orwell's durable contribution is the link between vague prose and vague thought. The practical upgrade is to force every draft to name actors, actions, evidence, tradeoffs, and uncertainty.

Skill rule:
- No sentence may hide agency when agency matters.
- If a sentence sounds impressive but does not name what changed, who did it, or why it matters, rewrite it.

### 2. Simplicity keeps the reader's energy on the idea

Paul Graham's argument is that ordinary words and simple sentences reduce friction. The point is not to dumb down the idea. The point is to keep the reader's cognitive budget on the substance.

Skill rule:
- Use the simplest word that preserves precision.
- Complicated syntax is allowed only when it carries complicated thought.
- Never make language fancy to cover an ordinary idea.

### 3. Concision means strongest words, not fewest words

Purdue's concision guidance distinguishes effective words from merely fewer words. The useful test is whether each word performs unique work.

Skill rule:
- Fire dead words.
- Replace vague multi-word phrases with exact nouns and verbs.
- Cut repetition unless rhythm, emphasis, or voice requires it.

### 4. Sentence variety creates life and emphasis

Purdue's sentence-variety guidance supports alternating length, rhythm, and openings. Monotone prose reads bland even when every sentence is grammatical.

Skill rule:
- Detect five consecutive sentences with similar length or structure.
- Use short sentences for impact.
- Use long sentences for accumulation, complexity, and momentum.

### 5. Active voice and hidden-verb removal harden prose

Digital.gov emphasizes active voice, present tense when accurate, and replacing nominalizations with verbs. This maps directly to anti-slop editing.

Skill rule:
- Prefer active voice when responsibility matters.
- Convert nominalizations into verbs.
- Use present tense when it shortens the sentence and remains accurate.

### 6. AI-detection heuristics are not the goal

Jakesch, Hancock, and Naaman show that human heuristics for AI-generated text can be flawed and manipulable. Therefore the skill should not chase detector tricks or add fake imperfections.

Skill rule:
- Do not intentionally add errors, quirks, contractions, slang, or fake vulnerability to seem human.
- Remove AI slop because it weakens the writing, not because a detector might flag it.
- Human voice comes from specific judgment, concrete context, and non-generic structure.

## Practical Upgrade

The skill should run every important draft through four gates:

1. Specificity: Is the writing grounded in concrete facts, mechanisms, stakes, and constraints?
2. Agency: Does it name who acts, decides, bears risk, or benefits?
3. Rhythm: Does the prose vary length, openings, pressure, and release?
4. Voice: Does it sound like the actual speaker, or like median internet prose?

Any draft that fails these gates is not done.
